PROJECT 03 / MACHINE LEARNING

Reading between the stars in customer reviews.

A team review analysis with a polarity baseline and a closer look at why keyword flags can misread unwanted sales pressure.

CIS 509 · Fall 2026Team coursework + fictional audit examples
Request project materials ↗

Team coursework · CIS 509 · Fall 2026 Team credited in the recovered progress deck: Christa Irakoze, Saurabh Bhatkar, Basilio Johnson.

The problem

Customer reviews can reveal service concerns, but a keyword mention is not proof of unwanted sales pressure. This project examines automotive reviews while making that distinction explicit.

Source-reported work

The technical progress deck reports filtering Yelp to Pennsylvania auto-repair businesses, sampling 800 businesses, matching 17,515 reviews, and retaining 16,735 reviews after a name-inference filter. These are review counts, not unique-customer counts. The original demographic inference was noisy and is excluded from the public demo.

The recovered baseline uses CountVectorizer + Multinomial Naive Bayes to predict rating polarity: 1–2 stars negative and 4–5 stars positive, excluding 3-star reviews. Reported holdout accuracy is 0.93 on 3,262 reviews. This evaluates rating polarity, not whether an upsell was unwanted. The reported majority-class accuracy is about 0.595.

The keyword baseline flagged 2,018 / 16,735 reviews (12.06%). Negation and ordinary recommendations can generate false positives. Historical metrics below are reproduced from the source deck, not rerun on Yelp here.

Public reconstruction

The demo runs a deliberately broad regex against six newly written fictional reviews, compares each flag with explicit illustrative labels, and exposes negation/context errors in keyword_audit.csv (available on request). It does not publish customer reviews or infer anyone's gender.

demo.py --input reviews.csv additionally fits the reconstructed count-vectorizer/Naive-Bayes pipeline to an authorized CSV with text and stars columns. Its evaluation uses a stratified review-level holdout; business/user grouping and temporal validation should be added before operational use. No Yelp data is redistributed.

What I would improve

Use human reference labels for unwanted pressure, quote evidence spans, and allow uncertain labels. Split by customer or shop to reduce repeated-entity leakage. Measure precision/recall for upsell detection separately from star polarity. A proposed LLM-agent extension appears in the progress deck, but no completed agent evaluation is claimed.

Evidence: CIS509_Automotive_Repair_Technical.pptx, source version 1, October 7, 2026, slides covering data filtering, regex detection, polarity model and validation gaps. An alternate notebook snapshot contains a missing-input-file error; neither that notebook nor raw reviews are represented here as successfully rerun.

Portfolio card

Contributed to a team NLP study of automotive reviews, combining data filtering, sentiment analysis, keyword features, and a Naive Bayes polarity baseline. Highlighted why keyword detection requires contextual validation and why demographic inference and repeated reviewers limit interpretation.
