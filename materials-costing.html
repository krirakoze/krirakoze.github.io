PROJECT 02 / FINANCIAL MODELING

What does a finished vest actually cost in materials?

Separate usable material, purchase requirements, and waste. Explore how price and waste assumptions change unit economics.

ASU capstone · Fall 2026Capstone-inspired · synthetic assumptions
View code & evidence ↗

Christa Irakoze · ASU DPR/OES capstone-inspired reconstruction · Fall 2026

The problem

A purchase price alone does not explain how much material a product should require. The direct-material model separates the usable quantity in one finished vest from purchased material lost as waste.

My contribution and scope

My capstone work focuses on direct materials within a team sourcing and financial-modeling project. This public reconstruction demonstrates the calculation with eight fictional component assumptions. It includes no supplier records or confidential purchasing data.

Gross quantity = net quantity / (1 − waste rate)

Component cost = gross quantity × price per matching unit

Waste cost = cost including waste − net material cost

At the recovered prototype assumptions, material cost is $5.433216 per vest, including $0.383216 of waste, versus $5.05 without waste. At 1,000 vests this is $5,433.22. These are synthetic cost calculations, not quoted prices or achieved savings.

Selected methods

  • Editable CSV assumptions with quantities, units, waste, and prices.
  • Row-level net and gross costs, total cost, and batch calculations.
  • A one-variable sensitivity analysis for mesh price and mesh waste.
  • Validation rejects negative quantities/prices and waste rates outside [0,1).

Interpretation

The gross-quantity formula avoids understating cost when waste is measured as a share of material purchased. A change in waste changes required purchases even if the finished vest uses the same net material. Quantities and prices must use matching units; changing m² to kg requires an actual conversion assumption.

Limits

Direct materials only. Labor, overhead, freight, tariffs, inventory holding, financing, margin, bulk discounts, pack rounding, and scrap recovery are excluded. The bill of materials is illustrative and not a verified manufacturer's design. Sponsor indexed sales/cost values are not actual dollar unit prices and do not calibrate this example. The demo does not claim a top-10 opportunity ranking or total should-cost.

Evidence: recovered OES_Vest_28633_Direct_Materials.xlsx, October 29, 2026. All eight quantities, prices, waste rates and batch size in that workbook are explicitly synthetic. This reconstruction omits its sponsor reference tab.

Portfolio card

Developed a direct-material costing prototype that separates net usage, gross purchased quantity, and waste cost. Added sensitivity scenarios to show how material prices and waste affect per-unit and batch costs using transparent, editable assumptions.
